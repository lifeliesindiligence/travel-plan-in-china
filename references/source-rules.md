# Source and freshness rules

## What proves an outing is possible

1. **Date-specific official notice, official booking page, or on-site status**: may establish opening, reservations, show times, and named attraction operation for the target date. Record the actual target date and retrieval time. A general “open daily” listing is weaker than a temporary closure notice.
2. **Government/meteorological/transit source**: use for weather, warnings, public transport and road access. A forecast is valid only for the date shown; refresh close to departure. Venue operation is still the venue's decision.
3. **Map or third-party ticket listing**: helpful for orientation and candidate services, but a generic schedule is not proof that an individual ride runs on the target date.
4. **Traveller posts/comments**: use for subjective feel, wayfinding, common friction and questions to check. Prices, free-entry policies, event schedules, queues, closures and re-entry rules in old posts are historical.

For important claims, record `source_id`, `observed_at`, `target_date`, and a status: `confirmed`, `historical`, or `unknown`. If two current sources conflict, show the conflict and choose an official date-specific source when available. Do not fill a gap with an invented number.

## Xiaohongshu collection

- Search `景点名 攻略` and `景点名 注意事项`. Add city, current season, crowd, parking, accessibility or rides only when relevant.
- Save the exact query, sort mode, timestamp, result IDs, engagement numbers, and exclusion reasons. Search results can be incomplete or ranked differently across queries; do not claim a global top ten from a local result page.
- Deduplicate by note ID. Separate **popular** from **recent** evidence. Use up to ten relevant notes/videos for each purpose when available; report the actual count when fewer exist.
- For caution comments, record how many first-level comments were loaded and how many were selected. “Top 30” means top 30 **within the loaded set** unless full pagination was verified. Look at recent and dissenting comments to avoid a like-only echo chamber.
- Keep a finding trace: claim → note ID/URL → post date → comment or video timestamp. Summarize themes; do not copy long passages into the deck.

## Video, images and publication

- Video narration is usable only when speech is present. A music montage can still provide visual examples, but its lyrics are not travel advice. For doubtful speech, review the clip or omit the claim.
- Use screenshots only from relevant moments, without presenting a different ride's image as the named attraction. Keep author/source links in the deck.
- A public skill repository contains code and examples, not user-downloaded videos, proprietary venue maps or credentials. Check the relevant source's permissions before redistributing its media or map data.
